# World Suicide Rate Report (2001–2021)

## 📌 Project Overview
This project analyzes global suicide rates per 100,000 persons over a 20‑year period (2001–2021).  
The analysis highlights demographic differences, country‑level disparities, and year‑on‑year changes.  
The goal is to provide actionable insights that can inform public health strategies and policy interventions.

## 🎯 Objectives
- Track global suicide mortality trends across two decades.
- Compare suicide rates by gender and age group.
- Measure year‑on‑year (YoY) percentage changes.
- Identify countries with the highest and lowest suicide rates.
- Provide recommendations for targeted interventions.

## 📊 Key Findings
- High variation in suicide rates across countries (CV = 66%).  
- Males accounted for **76%** of suicides, females for **24%**.  
- People aged **70+** had the highest suicide rate, while those aged **10–19** had the lowest.  
- Russia recorded the highest suicide rate (33.1), while Syria had the lowest (0.75).  
- A sharp **37% spike** occurred between 2020 and 2021, breaking the long‑term downward trend.

## 📝 Recommendations
- Develop male‑centric mental health programs to address stigma and barriers to help‑seeking.  
- Implement social connection initiatives to combat isolation.  
- Provide better pain management care for people aged 70+.  
- Investigate the 2020–2021 spike further to understand underlying causes.
